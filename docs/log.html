PROGRESS / UPDATED OCTOBER 29, 2026

From a plan
to a runnable prototype.

The project is now objectPilot: stationary object recognition and measured approach on RoboMaster TT. Our first target is a printed object image on a wall. The earlier person-following direction is retained here as history.

  1. EARLY PLANNING

    Initial concept and platform research

    The original idea used MediaPipe Pose for person following. Component research led to a RoboMaster TT with laptop processing over Wi-Fi. The onboard-compute and night-vision directions were set aside.

    Historical direction; it is not the current implementation.

    Recorded
  2. OCT 15–22

    Move from people to objects

    The October 15 plan established the course dates and expected November 4 arrival. The October 22 PRD moved the goal to detecting a stationary object, approaching it and comparing PPO with PID.

    The current scope removes human identity, person following, night operation and policy-controlled altitude.

    Complete
  3. OCT 29

    Rapid prototypes

    Built
    A Python package with YOLO inference, video/webcam dry runs, target confirmation, a shared 64-value observation, PID/PPO interfaces and an independent supervisor.
    Executed
    15 automated tests passed. A ten-sample blank-video run returned zero motion throughout. PPO training, saving, loading and evaluation completed.
    Measured
    On the same 30 simulated starts, initial PID completed 30 approaches; PPO after 100,352 transitions completed 14. Both recorded zero minimum-distance violations. These are development diagnostics, not flight results.
    Prepared
    Video extraction and grouped data splits, a printed-target collection procedure, and an ESP32 firmware extension exposing range validity and age.
    Limits
    No real printed-target accuracy measurements, TT flight tests, firmware compilation or flashing. The external ToF adapter is not enabled.
    Decision
    Keep PID as the baseline. Use matte wall prints for the first demonstration and verify the included forward ToF before adding a sensor.
    Replay the simulator ↗Inspect all results ↗
    Complete
  4. OCT 30–NOV 3

    Collect data and present the prototype

    Select the printed classes, record phone footage, label representative frames and benchmark the pretrained detector. Tune PID on validation scenarios and train multiple PPO seeds. Present the current prototype and its limits on November 3.

    Next
  5. NOV 4–7

    Expected arrival and hardware qualification

    Record delivered firmware versions, verify forward ToF against a tape, calibrate camera/sensor geometry and measure control response. Compile and test the prepared ESP32 extension. Flight dispatch, takeover, watchdog and link-loss behavior must pass hardware checks.

    Arrival is approximate. If it slips, preserve the calibration sequence and reduce optional features.

    Planned
  6. NOV 8–DEC 3

    Integrate, compare and freeze

    First validate a supervised PID approach to wall prints, then compare calibrated PPO candidates using matched conditions. Peer Review 1 is due November 10 at noon Pacific. Freeze features on December 3 and choose the most reliable validated demo controller.

    Planned
  7. DEC 4–15

    Rehearsal and final delivery

    Rehearse December 4–9. Final presentation and demonstration: December 10. Final report and Peer Review 2: December 15 at noon Pacific.

    Planned
